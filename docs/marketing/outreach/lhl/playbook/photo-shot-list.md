# Physical Book Photography — Shot List

Jeff has multiple physical books on hand, with a new one arriving Monday. Goal: capture 8–12 strong photos that fuel the press kit, sample preview supporting visuals, IG carousel posts, gift-guide submissions, and reply attachments when an influencer asks "got more pics?"

**Privacy rule:** **No identifiable child faces.** All shots either crop above the face, use over-the-shoulder framing, focus on hands, or shoot with adults only.

---

## Equipment recommendations

- Phone camera is fine if it's recent (iPhone 13+, Pixel 7+). DSLR/mirrorless is better but not required.
- Natural light wins. Late morning or late afternoon, near a large window. Avoid direct overhead sun.
- A simple white or warm-cream backdrop for the "studio" shots — a piece of poster board or a plain wall works.
- One reflector (or just a piece of white foam board) to bounce light into shadows on the studio shots.

---

## The 10 shots (priority order)

### Tier 1 — Must-have (5 shots)

**Shot 1: Cover hero, top-down, paper texture visible**
- Single book closed, on a warm-cream surface (linen tablecloth, paper texture, light wood).
- Top-down angle, book centered, ~10% margin around the book.
- Use as the primary product image on press kit and any "what does it look like?" reply.

**Shot 2: Open spread, slight angle, "looking into the book"**
- Book open to an emotionally strong spread (the forest "Look up" scene, or another inner-voice moment).
- Slight tilt — about 30° from vertical — so the viewer feels like they're being shown the book.
- Soft natural light from the side; some shadow under the book is fine, adds depth.
- Use in press kit and gift-guide submissions.

**Shot 3: Hands holding open book, kid-scale (no face)**
- Adult hands or older sibling hands holding the open book in their lap.
- Frame from below the chin down — face cropped out entirely.
- The book is the subject; hands frame it.
- Use this when influencers ask "what does it feel like to hold?"

**Shot 4: Multi-book stack with name visible**
- Stack 3–4 books with different personalized covers (different names + characters visible on the spines or top covers).
- Shoot from the side at a slight angle so all the names read.
- Use this to show "personalization variety" in the press kit and the sample-preview's last page.

**Shot 5: Reading scene over-the-shoulder (faceless)**
- Adult and child seated, reading the book together.
- Shoot from behind, over the adult's shoulder.
- Frame the book + the back of the child's head + the adult's hand turning a page.
- (We already have one of these — `docs/branding/photography/lifestyle-parent-child-reading-tiger.jpg`. Add 2–3 more in different lighting / locations to vary.)

### Tier 2 — Strong supporting shots (3 shots)

**Shot 6: Close-up of the personalized name on the cover**
- Macro / close-up of the cover, focused on the child's name.
- Soft bokeh background.
- Use as a hero image in social posts and DM attachments.

**Shot 7: Close-up of an inside-spread illustration detail**
- Tight crop of one beautifully illustrated detail — the tiger's face, the moonlight on a tree, the glow of the inner light.
- Shows the art quality up close.
- Use in gift-guide submissions and Instagram carousels.

**Shot 8: Book held by an adult hand, arm extended**
- Single-hand grip on the cover, arm extended, blurred neutral background.
- Conveys "this is something I'm holding up to show you."
- Use as the email-pitch attachment when offering "happy to send a photo if helpful."

### Tier 3 — Nice-to-have (2 shots)

**Shot 9: Gift-wrapped book**
- Wrapped in simple kraft paper with a ribbon, tagged. Aim for "thoughtful gift" energy.
- Use for holiday/birthday-gift-guide submissions.

**Shot 10: Book in the kid's space (no kid)**
- Book sitting on a child's nightstand, bookshelf, or play area, with toys or a stuffed animal nearby for context.
- "This belongs in their world."
- Use in lifestyle content and Substack newsletter mentions.

## Shooting tips

**Composition:**
- Leave breathing room around the subject. Tight crops feel cramped; generous margins feel premium.
- Rule of thirds for the angled shots.
- Top-down (also called "flat lay") works for product shots; eye-level works for in-the-wild shots.

**Color:**
- Warm tones (cream, wood, soft yellow) match brand. Avoid cold blue light.
- If shooting near a window, late morning gives the best warmth.
- Don't oversaturate in editing — the watercolor cover already pops.

**Editing:**
- Lightroom or Photoshop. Free alternatives: Snapseed (mobile), Affinity Photo, Photopea.
- Light edit only: slight warmth bump, lift the shadows a bit, don't crush blacks.
- Export to JPG, ~80% quality, max dimension 2400px.

**File naming:**
- `lifestyle-{description}.jpg` for in-the-wild
- `product-{description}.jpg` for studio shots
- e.g., `product-cover-topdown.jpg`, `lifestyle-stack-of-books.jpg`

**Storage:**
- All photos go to `docs/branding/photography/`. Update `asset-inventory.md` after each batch.
