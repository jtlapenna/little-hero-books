# 30-second product video — Approach guide (no kids' faces)

Jeff is a videographer but wants a clear approach that respects child privacy. This is the framework: a faceless, book-and-hands-and-screen-driven 30-second cut that shows the product, the personalization, and the emotional payoff without ever showing a child's face.

**Output target:** 30 seconds, 1080p (16:9 master), with a 9:16 vertical cut for IG Reels / TikTok.

---

## Concept

**The "inner voice" approach:** A first-person-ish edit that mirrors the book's theme. The viewer sees what a parent sees when they personalize and share the book — the screen, their hands, the cover appearing, the page-turn, the child's reaction (heard or implied, not shown).

Privacy + emotion in the same frame: we never show a face, but we *hear* the moment of recognition or *imply* it through hand placement, body language, and the book's reaction.

---

## 30-second structure

### Act 1 — The hook (0:00–0:05)
**Shots:**
- 0:00–0:02 — Tight shot of the screen: a name being typed letter by letter. ("Mateo" / "Aria")
- 0:02–0:04 — Cut to the cover preview appearing on screen with that name.
- 0:04–0:05 — Cut to a hand picking up the printed physical book (shipping packaging visible peripherally — "it just arrived" feel).

**On-screen text overlay (large, 36–48pt Poppins):**
- 0:00: "Type their name."
- 0:03: "Their story appears."

### Act 2 — The build (0:05–0:18)
**Shots:**
- 0:05–0:08 — Cover close-up. Personalized name visible. Hand holding it up against soft natural light.
- 0:08–0:11 — Page turn (audio: real page-turn sound). Inside spread reveal — slow.
- 0:11–0:14 — Tight crop on a detail in the illustration (the tiger's face, the glow, the moon).
- 0:14–0:18 — Wide-ish shot: book open in someone's lap (faceless, just hands and the book), gentle motion.

**On-screen text overlay:**
- 0:08: "Your child becomes the hero."
- 0:14: "Their look. Their animal. Their story."

### Act 3 — The emotional payoff (0:18–0:28)
**Shots:**
- 0:18–0:22 — Over-the-shoulder framing: parent and child together, book open, child's small hand reaching toward a page detail. **Frame everything below the chin or behind the head.**
- 0:22–0:26 — Audio: a child's giggle or a quiet "that's me!" reaction (recorded with permission, no face shown). If audio isn't available, use a gentle musical swell instead.
- 0:26–0:28 — Hand closes the book. Cover sits on a soft surface. Slight zoom out.

**On-screen text overlay:**
- 0:22: "Every child is the hero of their own story."

### Act 4 — The CTA (0:28–0:30)
**Shots:**
- 0:28–0:30 — Cut to clean end card: logo + URL + ASIN.

**On-screen elements:**
- Logo (centered)
- "littleherolabs.com" (smaller, below logo)
- "Available on Amazon Custom" (small footer)

---

## What we DON'T show

- No child's face. Period. Backs of heads, hair, hands — yes. Faces — no.
- No identifying details (no school logos, no street signs, no full names of real children written or said).
- No real product timeline promises ("arrives in 4 days!" — defer to checkout estimate).

---

## Audio plan

**Recommended:**
- Soft acoustic instrumental bed under the whole edit. Royalty-free from Epidemic Sound, Artlist, or Musicbed. Tone: warm, contemplative, hopeful. Think Studio Ghibli or Patty Griffin acoustic.
- Light ambient sound design: page turns (real, recorded), a soft cover-thud at the end, maybe a child's laugh in act 3 (with parent permission, no face on camera).
- No voiceover. The on-screen text + the visuals carry the meaning.

**Alternative (if voiceover is preferred):**
- Founder voice (Jeff). One short line, mid-act-3:
  > "Every child becomes the hero of their own story."
- That's it. No intro, no CTA in voice.

---

## Camera + lighting notes

- Natural window light. Late morning or golden hour.
- Shoot at 24fps for the cinematic feel; export at 30fps for social.
- 50mm or equivalent prime for the close-ups; 24–35mm for wider shots.
- For the over-the-shoulder shot in act 3, shoot tight enough that the child's face is *physically out of frame*, not just out-of-focus. Crop in-camera, not in post.

---

## Editing tips

- Pace: Acts 1 and 4 are quick; act 2 has space; act 3 is the longest beat (the emotional landing).
- Subtle camera moves are fine (slow push-in, slight rack focus). Avoid handheld shake — feels off-brand for warmth.
- Color grade: warm, slightly soft. Don't crush blacks. Lift the shadows just a bit. The watercolor cover already pops; let it.
- Match cuts: name appearing on screen → cover with the same name appearing in hands. Visual rhyme adds polish.
- Music ducks under any audio reaction in act 3.

## Why this approach (one paragraph)

Showing a child's face triggers a specific cognitive response in the viewer: it pulls focus to the child as the subject. We don't want that. We want focus on the *book* and the *moment*. By staying faceless, the viewer's imagination fills in the gap with their own kid. That's a stronger conversion driver than any face we could show. It's also the right call for child privacy, FTC guidelines, and cleaner usage rights for any creator we partner with.
