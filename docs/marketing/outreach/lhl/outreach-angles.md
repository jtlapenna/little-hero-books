# LHL outreach angles

A short library of named angles R2 picks from when drafting per-creator pitches. Each angle is a way of framing the *why this product fits this creator* point. The angle drives the body of the pitch; the channel-specific format (read-aloud / unboxing / gift guide / etc.) drives the phrasing tweaks already documented in the template files.

Pair each draft with exactly one angle. R2 records the `angle_tag` in the draft frontmatter and in `message-library.md`.

If a creator doesn't fit any of these angles cleanly, R2 should flag them for Jeff rather than invent a new angle on the fly. New angles get added here intentionally, not in passing.

---

## Angle catalog

| `angle_tag` | Use when the creator is… | Lead-with framing |
|---|---|---|
| `librarian-kidlit-curator` | A librarian, kidlit reviewer, picture-book curator, or recommendation account | Curatorial fit. The book belongs in their stack of recommended titles because of art quality, story arc, and personalization category. |
| `literacy-educator` | An educator, literacy specialist, SLP, homeschooler, Montessori-aligned | Developmental and emotional-literacy fit. The story is about a child finding their inner voice; aligned with what their audience already cares about. |
| `diverse-representation` | A creator focused on diverse / multicultural / LGBTQ+ / inclusive kidlit | Representation fit. Most personalized books pick from pre-made characters; this product lets every child see themselves on the page (skin tone, hair, animal, pronouns). |
| `read-aloud-storytime` | A read-aloud / storytime / family-reading creator (often parents or two-parent duos) | Visual + emotional moment. Kids light up when they see themselves on the page; the favorite-animal reveal is the on-camera beat. |
| `gift-curator` | A gift-roundup curator, Amazon-finds editor, holiday-gift Substack | Gift fit. Personalized, made-to-order, watercolor-style illustration, $29.99 launch price, fits birthdays / new-baby / grandparent gifting. |

## Angle: `librarian-kidlit-curator`

### Public signals that justify this angle
- Bio mentions: librarian, MLS, school librarian, public library, kidlit, picture-book recs
- Posts patterns: weekly book lists, "books for X" roundups, review-style captions, age recommendations
- Bookshelf or stack imagery in posts
- Long-form review writing (Substack, blog, dedicated review handle)

### Lead-with framing (one paragraph for body)
The book belongs in a curated kidlit recommendation. *Finding Our Inner Voice* is a 16-page magical-journey story for ages 0–7, watercolor-style art, a clean emotional arc about quiet self-discovery. The personalization is not a gimmick: hair style, skin tone, hometown, all show up across the story. Their favorite animal joins for the final reveal.

### Safe personalization phrasing
- "Curated kidlit list" / "picture-book recs" / "your stack"
- Reference their format honestly: "for your weekly recs," "a fit for your roundups," "alongside the titles you've been highlighting"

### Claims to avoid
- Don't claim the book wins awards we haven't won
- Don't claim it's "the best personalized book on the market"
- Don't compare against named librarian-favorite titles unfavorably

### CTA shape
Gifted copy → see if it earns a recommendation. No pressure beyond honest reaction.

## Angle: `literacy-educator`

### Public signals that justify this angle
- Bio mentions: M.Ed, M.A. Literacy, teacher, SLP, homeschool, Montessori, play-based, early childhood, ECE
- Posts patterns: developmental milestones, vocabulary tips, emotional regulation, executive function, classroom-ready content
- Educator credentials on display

### Lead-with framing
The story is built around a developmental beat: a child finding their own inner voice. Themes are emotional intelligence, courage, friendship, and quiet wisdom. It's the kind of book that pairs with what they already teach: emotional literacy, self-talk, calm-down strategies.

### Safe personalization phrasing
- "Your audience already cares about emotional literacy"
- "Aligned with what you teach about [self-regulation / inner voice / kindness]"
- Reference their professional credential when present and earned (M.Ed, SLP, etc.)

### Claims to avoid
- Don't claim measurable educational outcomes (no "improves reading scores," no "research-backed")
- Don't promise the book is curriculum-aligned
- Don't co-opt their professional language for marketing

### CTA shape
Gifted copy for their child or classroom. Honest reaction welcome.

## Angle: `diverse-representation`

### Public signals that justify this angle
- Bio or handle mentions: Black, biracial, multicultural, diverse, inclusive, LGBTQ+, queer, indigenous, BIPOC, kidlit
- Posts patterns: representation-focused recs, mirror-window-door framing, identity-affirming content
- Account history of advocating for kids seeing themselves in books

### Lead-with framing
Most personalized books let parents pick from pre-made characters. We let them create their own. Hair style, skin tone, hometown, pronouns all show up across the story. Their favorite animal joins them for the final reveal. Every child gets to be the actual hero, not a closest-match.

### Safe personalization phrasing
- "Every child seeing themselves on the page"
- "Beyond pre-made characters"
- Reference their advocacy work directly when relevant

### Claims to avoid
- Don't claim the book solves representation problems
- Don't position the product as a substitute for #ownvoices kidlit
- Don't conflate customization with cultural authenticity (the book is one tool, not the whole answer)

### CTA shape
Gifted copy → if it lands for them, share more about partnership.

## Angle: `read-aloud-storytime`

### Public signals that justify this angle
- Bio mentions: storytime, read-aloud, bedtime stories, family reading, two-parent / family duo accounts
- Posts patterns: parent reading to child on camera, kid reactions, snuggle-time content, mid-read commentary
- High kid-on-screen content with positive emotional energy

### Lead-with framing
The on-camera moment is when a kid sees themselves on the page for the first time. The favorite-animal reveal in the final two pages is the surprise beat that tends to land on video. The story's emotional arc (quiet, gentle, courage-affirming) reads naturally aloud.

### Safe personalization phrasing
- "Read-along moment with your kid as the hero"
- "First-reaction beat on camera"
- "Bedtime-story format"

### Claims to avoid
- Don't promise viral content
- Don't script the kid's reaction for them
- Don't promise emotional response (some kids will be shy on camera)

### CTA shape
Gifted personalized copy → film however feels natural to their style.

## Angle: `gift-curator`

### Public signals that justify this angle
- Bio or content theme: gift roundups, Amazon finds, holiday lists, baby-shower picks, grandparent gifts, ShopLTK affiliate
- Posts patterns: "best gifts for [age]" lists, seasonal holiday content, registry recs

### Lead-with framing
Made-to-order personalized children's book, $29.99 launch price, ships in 3–5 business days via Lulu. Fits birthdays, baby showers, grandparent gifting, new-sibling milestones. Watercolor-style art, premium 8.5×8.5 softcover, ages 0–7.

### Safe personalization phrasing
- "For your gift roundups"
- "Could be a fit for your [holiday / season / theme] picks"
- "Editorial mention or full review, your call"

### Claims to avoid
- Don't quote price points that aren't current ($29.99 launch only; no $24.99 promo unless actively running)
- Don't promise inventory holds for editorial timing
- Don't claim Amazon Choice or similar marketing labels we don't have

### CTA shape
Editorial mention or gifted personalized copy. Either path.

## How R2 picks an angle

1. Read the creator's row in `pipeline.md` (niche, bio summary, content type).
2. Match against the public-signals list above. The first matching angle wins. If two match equally, prefer the one with stronger signal density (e.g., a librarian who also runs gift guides → `librarian-kidlit-curator` because the librarian credential is the stronger trust anchor).
3. If no angle matches confidently, **flag the creator for Jeff** rather than inventing an angle. Note the mismatch in `copy-review.md`.

R2 records the chosen angle in:
- The draft file frontmatter (`angle_tag: librarian-kidlit-curator`)
- `message-library.md` (variant entry)
- `pipeline.md` Notes column
