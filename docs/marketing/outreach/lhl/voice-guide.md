# LHL outreach voice guide

Derived from Jeff's reactions to the 9 tone-co-creation drafts. These are the things that **felt like Jeff** when he read them. Apply to every outreach draft.

---

## Core principles

**1. Direct. No beating around the bush.**
Get to the point in the first paragraph. Don't build up to it.

> ✅ "I'm Jeff, founder of Little Hero Labs. We make personalized kids' books where your child is literally the hero on every page. Open to a gifted collab?"

> ❌ "Your essay has been rattling around my head for a week. The bit about repetition is something I keep coming back to as a parent. I'm Jeff…"

**2. Transparent about the business context.**
We're running campaigns. We're looking for partners. We have an offer we'll share if there's interest *and* the audience is right. Don't pretend this is a personal note from a friend who happened to make a book.

> ✅ "We're running a few partner campaigns right now. If it's a fit, I'll share more about our campaign offers."

> ❌ Anything that hides that this is outbound marketing.

**3. Outcome-framed product description, not mechanical.**
Talk about what the parent gets, not what the system does.

> ✅ "Your child becomes the hero of a personalized picture book."
> ✅ "Your child shows up on every page, illustrated to look like them."

> ❌ "Parents build the actual character." *(too mechanical)*
> ❌ "Hair style, skin tone, favorite color, and animal sidekick." *(reads as a feature list)*

**4. Authentic, not content-creator-corny.**
Skip the cute "I do X, you do Y" framing.

> ❌ "I'd cover the book, you cover the magic." *(corny)*

> ✅ "I'd send a personalized copy, and you'd film the read-aloud however feels natural."

**5. Brief, but complete.**
Aim for 100–180 words for emails, 60–100 for DMs. Long enough to be clear, short enough to actually get read.

**6. Wonderbly comparison: optional, A/B test.**
Naming Wonderbly directly is uncertain. Some readers will appreciate the clear contrast, others might find it slightly aggressive. Default: don't name competitors. A/B test variants that do.

> ✅ Default: "Most personalized books pick from pre-made characters. Ours doesn't."
> 🤷 A/B variant: "Wonderbly lets parents pick from pre-made characters. We let them create their own."

**7. Signal that there's a partnership opportunity, but not the mechanics.**
Be clear there's a real partnership conversation behind the gifted offer. Don't share mechanism (rev share, flat fee) or dollar amounts until interest and audience quality are confirmed.

> ✅ "Open to a gifted collab? If it's a fit, we can talk through partnership details from there."

> ❌ "Open to a gifted collab? Typically pays a custom discount code rev share or a small flat fee." *(too specific too early)*

## Standard openers (default, no specific-reference required)

Use these as the default. They lead with who we are and what we have. Don't say "Quick pitch" as a preamble; just start with the intro.

```
Hi [Name],

I'm Jeff, founder of Little Hero Labs. We make personalized children's books where your child becomes the hero of the story, illustrated to look like them, with their animal companion at their side.
```

```
Hi [Name],

Jeff here from Little Hero Labs. We make personalized picture books for ages 0–7 where your child is the hero of the story, illustrated to look like them.
```

```
Hi [Name],

I'm Jeff at Little Hero Labs. We make personalized picture books where your child becomes the hero of a magical-journey story, illustrated to look like them, with their animal companion at their side.
```

## Specific-reference opener (A/B variant only, don't default)

For some creators, a specific reference signals real attention and outperforms the generic opener. For others (Jeff included as a recipient), it reads as cold-outreach-marketing artifice. Default: skip it. A/B test it explicitly when piloting.

If using:
- Reference must be **actually specific**. A phrase from a recent post, a recurring theme, a named episode. Not "love your content!" or "your work is amazing."
- Keep it under one sentence.
- Pivot to the founder intro within 2 lines.

## Standard framing of the product

Use these. Vary across drafts so a recipient seeing two different pitches doesn't see the same sentence twice.

> "Your child is the hero on every page."
> "Your child shows up on every spread, illustrated to look like them."
> "Your child becomes the hero of the story, illustrated to look like them."
> "It's a personalized picture book where your kid becomes the lead character."
> "A keepsake picture book starring your child, illustrated to look like them."
> "Your child is literally the protagonist. Their look, their animal companion, their hometown all show up across the story."

## Standard partnership/offer mention

Pick one per draft. Always **lead with gifted, not paid**. **Do not name any offer mechanism, structure, or dollar amount in the first message.** Gauge interest and audience quality first; share specifics only when both are confirmed.

> "We're running partner campaigns this quarter and looking for a few more creators. Would you be open to a gifted collab? I'd send you a personalized copy for your family, no commitment beyond your honest reaction. If you like the book, I'll share more about our campaign offers."

> "We're running partner campaigns this quarter. Happy to make a personalized copy for your child first so you can see it before deciding. If you enjoy the book, I'll share details about our partner program."

> "Starting point is always a gifted book, no strings. If the book lands for you, I can share more about how the campaigns work and what we offer."

**Rule of thumb:** the first message **never** names the offer mechanism, structure, or dollar amounts. Lead with the gifted book. If the creator expresses interest after receiving it, that's the signal to internally assess audience fit and decide whether to extend an offer. The mechanism (rev share, flat fee) and dollar amounts go in a follow-up message *after* both interest and audience quality are confirmed.

**Why:** sharing offer specifics upfront commits us to mechanics before we know the audience is right, and signals that this is a transactional pitch rather than a gauging-fit conversation. Holding the offer for after the book lands lets us assess audience quality from the creator's response and audience signals in the meantime.

## Sign-off

Always include the URL so the recipient can verify the brand quickly. Don't lead the sign-off with an em dash.

**Default (use everywhere, DMs and emails):**

```
Jeff
Little Hero Labs | littleherolabs.com
```

**Optional alternates:**

```
Jeff
Founder, Little Hero Labs
littleherolabs.com
```

```
Jeff (Founder)
Little Hero Labs | littleherolabs.com
```

For email, the pipe-separated single-line version reads cleanly. For DMs, same. The URL belongs in every signature.

## Format rules

**No em dashes anywhere.** Use periods, commas, colons, or parentheses instead. This is a hard rule. The em-dash character is a strong AI tell and Jeff has flagged it explicitly.

**Proper line breaks.** Every email and long DM has paragraph breaks between distinct ideas. No walls of text. Two-line paragraph max for DMs, three for emails.

**Humanizer pass on every batch.** Run [`humanizer`](../../../../.claude/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md) before presenting drafts to Jeff. Specifically check:
- AI vocabulary (delve, leverage, tapestry, pivotal, intricate, underscore, garner, fostering)
- Repetitive sentence openers across the batch
- Negative parallelisms ("It's not just X, it's Y")
- Rule-of-three flourishes when 1–2 items would do
- Curly quotes vs straight quotes
- Em dashes (sneaky, they show up in template content)

**Subject lines (email):** Concrete and specific to the ask. No clickbait. No em dashes in subject lines either. Use a comma or "from" or just rephrase.

> ✅ "Personalized kids book, gifted collab?"
> ✅ "Quick partnership pitch from Little Hero Labs"
> ✅ "Gifted book offer for [Channel Name]"
> ❌ "Personalized kids book — gifted collab?" (em dash)

## A/B testing approach

Test one dimension at a time. Don't compound variables. With two in flight at once, you can't tell which drove the difference.

**Phase 1 (first ~20 sent pitches): opener style.**
- Default: direct founder intro
- Variant: specific-reference opener (must be actually specific, not generic praise)
- Compare response rates. Switch default only if variant beats default by ≥50% on response rate.

**Phase 2 (next ~20 pitches): Wonderbly comparison.**
- Default: no comparison invoked
- Variant: name Wonderbly directly ("Wonderbly lets parents pick from pre-made characters. We let them create their own.")
- Same comparison method.

**Recording the variant:** in `pipeline.md` notes column, mark each row with `variant: direct` or `variant: specific-ref` (and later `wonderbly: named` or `wonderbly: not`).

Once both phases close, lock the winners as default. Re-test annually or when audience shifts.

## What "sounds like Jeff"

Reading the drafts back, the ones that landed felt:
- Direct (gets to the point)
- Brief (didn't over-explain)
- Honest about the business motivation (we're running partnerships, this isn't a personal note pretending otherwise)
- Outcome-focused (your kid becomes the hero, not "we let you build a character")
- Real about the offer (we have a structure, we'll share if interested)

Drafts that didn't land felt:
- Performative (specific-reference opener that read as researched-then-targeted)
- Corny (cute "I do X, you do Y" framing)
- Mechanical (talking about what the system lets you do, not what the parent gets)
- Long (founder-letter style took too many paragraphs to get anywhere)
