## Polling benchmark implications

The retained PDFs are approximately 1.9–2.0 MB per child, generated through the configured assembler with visible dummy identities. A successful single check downloads about 3.8–4.0 MB (our original + provider copy); siblings download about 7.76 MB. Fast Node trials and the focused Worker are local estimates. Simulated 10 ms/64 KiB body delivery remained below the default 2.5-second candidate budget; a 100 ms/64 KiB sibling case timed out at approximately 2.5 seconds with zero status applies. Exact measurements are in the receipt.

The Worker bridge buffers response bodies. Its numbers do not measure real R2/Gelato latency, deployed OpenNext overhead, billed CPU, peak memory, cold-start guarantees or final illustration sizes. Node process CPU/RSS includes the harness and is not Worker billing evidence. This benchmark checks one candidate per route invocation; it does not measure a full three-candidate batch or prove sustainable fleet throughput. The separate fairness suite covers seven queued candidates in three ticks synthetically.

At one check every five minutes, the measured byte totals imply roughly **1.09–1.15 GB per single-child order per day** or **2.24 GB per sibling submission per day**, assuming continuous polling and unchanged files. These are arithmetic estimates, not observed traffic or money. A three-candidate cap also bounds throughput to at most three candidates per trigger; a five-minute trigger would serve at most 864 candidate attempts/day before failures or timeouts. No such schedule was enabled. Cadence, backoff, active cohort size and any safe optimization of repeated byte verification need review before ordinary activation. Do not silently remove integrity checks for speed.
