# Pressure Point Decisions - 2026-06-04

## Purpose

This captures the first workshop pass on the main story pressure points in the 30-page beat map.

These are story decisions, not final copy. The exact language can still change during drafting.

## 1. Page 7 - Top-Of-Slide Overwhelm

Locked direction: **big-but-contained overwhelm**.

The child wants to try, but the height, waiting, and other kids' voices become too much. The child is not failing. Their body is sending information they do not yet know how to understand.

Tone:

- not too mild
- not a full panic/breakdown
- body-based, kid-readable, and emotionally real

Working language:

> At the top, the slide looked longer than it had from the ground.
> "Your turn!" called one kid.
> "It's fun!" called another.
> The child's tummy flipped.
> Their hands held the rail.
> Their feet said, not yet.

## 2. Bedroom-To-Dream Transition / First Voice

Locked direction: the first voice moment should begin with **recognition**, not advice.

The child remembers the slide in a quiet, safe place. The tiny voice first names what happened:

> "That was scary."

The child responds:

> "Yes."

Then the voice realizes it has been heard:

> "Oh!" said the tiny voice. "You can hear me now."

The child should ask:

> "Who said that?"

The transition out of the bedroom should feel safe and whimsical, not spooky. Avoid the "quiet door opened just a crack" feeling if it reads like a haunted-house door.

Preferred transition image:

- a tiny light blinks on beside the bed
- then another
- then another
- like a path made of stars beginning inside the room

Open exact invitation line:

- "Let's look together."
- "I can show you."
- "You can follow if you want."

Avoid lines that sound like the child is being pulled away from safety.

## 3. Loud / Noisy Distinguish Scene

Locked direction: **playful, chaotic, confusing, not scary**.

The dream voices should mirror the playground pressure, but in magical kid-language. The voices are energetic and silly, not threatening.

The child should actively find the inner voice inside the noise.

Working beat:

> The stars started blinking in every direction.
> "This way!" blinked one.
> "No, this way!" puffed the wind.
> "Hop!" said a mushroom.
> "Skip!" said a stone.
> "Hurry!" pointed a sign with very important arrows.
>
> Every voice sounded sure.
> Every voice sounded busy.
>
> The child stopped.
> The child listened extra close.
>
> Under the windy voice,
> under the blinking voice,
> under the very important arrows,
> the tiny voice was still there.
>
> "Listen close," said the tiny voice. "I'm still here."

Transition:

The child finds the quiet path, and then the shiny shortcut appears beside it.

## 4. Shiny Shortcut

Locked direction: **tempting with a slight wrong-feeling edge**.

The shortcut should not be silly-only or spooky-only. It should be appealing because the child wants the scary feeling to go away, but something inside says no.

Working beat:

> A bright path blinked on.
> "FAST WAY!" it flashed.
> "NO SCARY FEELINGS!"
>
> That sounded nice.
> Very nice.
>
> But under the sparkle, the child felt one tiny no.

Story job:

This is the first active gut-check. The child distinguishes flashy/loud from quiet/true.

## 5. Warbly / Self-Critic / Untangle Scene

Locked direction: the child faces something genuinely unsafe or not-right-for-now.

The voice first sounds sharp or critical:

> "You can't do it."

The child does not overpower or ignore the sharp voice. The child uses the earlier tool:

- pause
- breathe
- listen closer

Then the sharp words soften, open, or untangle.

Underneath:

> "I'm scared."
> "Not this way."

Working visual:

- big prickly "YOU CAN'T" words on the cave wall
- the child breathes
- the prickly letters loosen
- inside them are smaller softer words: "I'm scared. Not this way."

Core lesson:

Self-criticism can be a protective message in a harsh costume. The child learns to listen under the sharpness instead of being crushed by it.

## 6. Glowing Stones / Trust Practice

Locked direction: use **tiny no / not yet / little yes** as the decision language.

The child practices noticing body-feelings that guide choice. The correct choice is not the biggest, brightest, or fastest stone. It is the one that feels warm/okay inside.

Working beat:

> Some stones were bright.
> Some were dim.
> Some wobbled in the air.
>
> "Which one?" asked the child.
>
> "Listen," said the tiny voice.
>
> The first stone felt like, not that one.
> The next stone felt like, not yet.
>
> Then one small stone glowed warm.
> The child felt a little yes.

Note:

Avoid "listen for one step." It does not read naturally enough.

## 7. Joy / Confidence

Locked direction: **running through wonder with almost-animal joy**.

The feeling after trusting the inner voice should read as freedom, confidence, and joy. This page should not introduce a new lesson. It should show the effect of listening and choosing.

Working beat:

> The child stepped onto the warm stone.
> Then another warm stone appeared.
> Then another.
>
> Soon the child was running.
> Not because anyone shouted hurry.
> Because their feet felt free.
>
> The world opened wide.
> A tail flashed between giant flowers.
> The child laughed.
> The quiet voice felt close now.

## 8. Animal Reveal

Locked direction: use a dialogue-based reveal.

The child realizes the animal was present at earlier moments, but the animal should not become the authority. The animal helped the child listen; the voice belongs to the child.

Preferred reveal structure:

> "It was you!" said the child.
> "At the door? In the wind? In the cave?"
>
> "A little," said the animal. "I helped you listen."
>
> "Then whose voice was it?" asked the child.
>
> The animal smiled.
> "Yours."

This is the current best direction, though exact language can still be softened or refined during copy drafting.
