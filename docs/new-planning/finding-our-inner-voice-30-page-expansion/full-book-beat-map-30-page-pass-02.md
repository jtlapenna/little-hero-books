# Full Book Beat Map - 30-Page Pass 02

Status: superseded as the current working map by `full-book-beat-map-31-page-pass-01.md`. Keep this file as planning history for the 30-page structure.

## Purpose

This pass incorporates the locked pressure-point decisions from `pressure-points-decisions-2026-06-04.md` into the full 30-page structure.

This is still a story-first beat map, not final copy and not an asset plan. The goal is to make the page sequence clear enough that the next step can be rough page copy.

## What Changed From Pass 01

- Replaced the spooky-feeling quiet door transition with a safer star/light path that begins inside the bedroom.
- Made the first voice moment about recognition: "That was scary."
- Made the noisy/distinguish section playful and chaotic, not scary.
- Gave the child active agency in finding the tiny voice inside the noise.
- Clarified the shiny shortcut as tempting but wrong-feeling.
- Reframed the cave/self-critic scene around a genuinely unsafe or not-right-for-now choice.
- Changed the trust-practice language from "one step" to **tiny no / not yet / little yes**.
- Made joy feel like freedom, confidence, and movement through wonder.
- Updated the animal reveal so the animal helps the child listen, but the voice belongs to the child.

## Page Beat Map

| Page | Section | Story Beat | Emotional / Lesson Job | Transition |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Front matter | Title page: *Finding Our Inner Voice*. | Sets the quiet, magical tone. | Turn into front matter. |
| 2 | Front matter | Blank page. | Gives dedication a right-hand page. | Sets up dedication. |
| 3 | Front matter | Personalized dedication. | Makes the book feel made for the child before story begins. | Turn into the first narrative spread. |
| 4 | Park setup | The child arrives at the park. The park is alive with swings, running feet, laughter, bright movement, and the custom child clearly present as the hero. | Grounds the story in a real kid situation and starts with life/energy, not fear. | The child sees many things to try. |
| 5 | Park setup | So many choices: swings, climbing, sandbox, games. The tall slide is visible, but it has not taken over yet. | Builds excitement and curiosity before uncertainty enters. | The slide becomes the brightest/loudest fun in the park. |
| 6 | Park setup | Kids whoosh down the tall slide, laugh, and run back for another turn. The child looks up. It looks fun. It also looks too big. | Introduces mixed feelings: wanting and uncertainty at the same time. | The child climbs or reaches the top. |
| 7 | Park setup | At the top, the slide looks longer than it did from the ground. "Your turn!" "It's fun!" The child's tummy flips, hands hold the rail, and feet say not yet. | Big-but-contained overwhelm. The child is not failing; their body is sending information they cannot yet understand. | Cut gently home without overexplaining the logistics of leaving the slide. |
| 8 | Remember / Notice | The child is home in bed or a quiet room. The slide is not in the room anymore, but the big feeling is. The loud playground voices bump around in memory. | Clarity begins when the child is safe and quiet enough to feel what happened. | The loud voices fade enough for a smaller sound to appear. |
| 9 | Remember / Notice | A tiny voice says, "That was scary." The child nods or whispers, "Yes." The voice answers, "Oh! You can hear me now." The child asks, "Who said that?" | The first voice moment is recognition, not advice. The child hears their own feeling named. | The child pauses, breathes, and the voice becomes easier to hear. |
| 10 | Pause / Breathe / Listen | A tiny light blinks on beside the bed. Then another. Then another. Like a path made of stars beginning inside the safe room. The voice offers a gentle invitation: "Let's look together" or similar. | The dream transition feels safe, chosen, and whimsical. Breathing/listening makes the path visible. | The star path leads out of the room as the dream grows around the child. |
| 11 | Follow | The bedroom softens into a night path. The child follows the star lights. The voice stays warm and nearby, inviting the child to move slowly. | The child starts trusting the voice enough to move, without being pulled or rushed. | A clue appears exactly where the next light should be. |
| 12 | Follow | First animal clues appear: tracks, a feather, fur, a tail, or a shadow. The child looks for the speaker but sees only the clue. | Builds the animal mystery and makes the unseen voice feel present. | The child follows the clues deeper into the dream. |
| 13 | Follow / humor | A playful almost-animal moment: a bush wiggles, sneezes, says "oops," or tries badly to hide. The child almost laughs. | Adds kid-language humor before the heavier middle and keeps the guide relational. | The funny quiet is interrupted by a burst of louder dream voices. |
| 14 | Distinguish | The dream becomes playful chaos. Stars blink in different directions, wind puffs advice, a mushroom says "Hop!", a stone says "Skip!", and a sign points very important arrows. | Mirrors the playground pressure without making the scene scary. The child experiences too many voices at once. | Every voice sounds sure, and the child cannot immediately find the tiny one. |
| 15 | Distinguish | The child stops and listens extra close. Under the windy voice, the blinking voice, and the very important arrows, the tiny voice is still there: "Listen close. I'm still here." The child finds the quiet path. | The child actively distinguishes their inner voice from the crowd. | A bright shortcut appears beside the quiet path. |
| 16 | Distinguish / Trust seed | A shiny shortcut flashes: "FAST WAY!" "NO SCARY FEELINGS!" It sounds nice. Very nice. But under the sparkle, the child feels one tiny no. | First active gut-check. The child distinguishes appealing/loud from quiet/true. | The child chooses the quieter path instead, and it leads toward the cave. |
| 17 | Untangle | The quiet path reaches a cave or dark passage with something genuinely unsafe or not-right-for-now, such as a broken bridge, too-wide jump, or slippery ledge. A sharp voice snaps, "You can't do it." | The inner voice sounds critical because fear is wrapped around a protective message. | The child hears the sharp words echo and feels the old tight slide feeling. |
| 18 | Untangle | The cave repeats, "You can't. You can't. You can't." The child does not like the voice; it sounds mean. But the child remembers how to pause, breathe, and listen. | The child does not overpower or ignore self-criticism. They use the earlier listening tool in a harder moment. | The prickly words begin to change. |
| 19 | Untangle | Big prickly "YOU CAN'T" words on the cave wall loosen, soften, or open. Inside them are smaller words: "I'm scared." | The child learns that sharp self-criticism can hide a feeling underneath. | The message becomes even clearer. |
| 20 | Untangle | The softened message finishes: "Not this way." The child realizes the sharp voice was trying to help. The cave brightens or a safer path opens. | Emotional core: fear is not defeated; it is understood. The child trusts a boundary instead of forcing bravery. | The safer path leads to a field of glowing stones. |
| 21 | Trust / Practice | The child reaches glowing stones. Some are bright, some dim, some wobbly. The child asks, "Which one?" The voice says, "Listen." | Sets up active practice. The child is not told which choice is right. | The child checks each stone against their inside feeling. |
| 22 | Trust / Practice | The first stone feels like not that one. The next feels like not yet. Then one small stone glows warm, and the child feels a little yes. The child steps onto it, and another warm stone appears. | Trust becomes action. The child practices tiny no / not yet / little yes. | Choosing from the little yes opens the world into joy. |
| 23 | Joy / Confidence | Soon the child is running through wonder: giant flowers, bright air, open space. Not because anyone shouted hurry, but because their feet feel free. A tail flashes between flowers. The child laughs. | Trusting the inner voice feels like freedom, confidence, and joy. The almost-animal flash carries momentum into the reveal. | This time, the tail does not disappear. |
| 24 | Bond / Reveal | The animal finally steps out. The child recognizes the tracks, feather, tail, shadow, sneeze, and voice. "It was you!" the child says. "By my bed? In the wind? In the cave?" | The animal reveal pays off the mystery and earlier clues. | The animal answers in a way that keeps the child's inner voice central. |
| 25 | Bond / Reveal | "A little," says the animal. "I helped you listen." The child asks, "Then whose voice was it?" The animal smiles: "Yours." The child and animal fly, walk, or glow home together. | The guide is not an outside authority. The child owns the wisdom. | The child returns ready to face the slide with a process, not a forced answer. |
| 26 | Return payoff | Back at the slide, the outside voices start again. The child feels the old tummy flip. This time, they pause and listen. They say, "You go first. I'm almost ready." | Real-world climax. The child makes space instead of freezing or rushing. | The child waits for their own little yes. |
| 27 | Return payoff | The child watches, breathes, and listens. When the little yes comes: one scoot, one whoosh. Or a smaller chosen action if final copy keeps the ending more open. | The payoff is confidence from listening and choosing, not forced fearlessness. | Story closes; end matter begins. |
| 28 | End matter | Spiral breathing / finger-trace exercise: pause, breathe, listen. Include the story language: tiny no, not yet, little yes. | Gives the child a repeatable tool from the story. | Turns from story into practice. |
| 29 | End matter | About / parent prompt: short Little Hero Labs note plus a prompt such as, "Can you remember a tiny no, a not yet, or a little yes?" | Gives parents a way to extend the book without turning the narrative into a lesson page. | Final blank/copyright page. |
| 30 | Back matter | Blank, copyright, or quiet brand note. | Gives the book a clean close. | End. |

## Lesson Compression

The 18-page dream arc now compresses the lesson ladder this way:

- Remember / Notice: pages 8-9
- Pause / Breathe / Listen: page 10
- Follow: pages 11-13
- Distinguish: pages 14-16
- Untangle: pages 17-20
- Trust / Practice: pages 21-22
- Joy / Confidence: page 23
- Bond / Reveal: pages 24-25

This version gives more room to Follow and Distinguish because those sections make the animal mystery and the "find the voice inside the noise" lesson work. Pause / Breathe / Listen is more compressed because it repeats later in the cave and return-to-slide scenes.

## Copy Draft Priorities

When drafting rough page copy, prioritize:

1. **Kid-language body cues**: tummy flip, hands on rail, feet saying not yet, warm stone, little yes.
2. **Active listening**: the child finds the voice; the child chooses; the child owns the wisdom.
3. **Safe dream transition**: no spooky doorway, no sense of being lured away.
4. **No villainizing fear**: fear is useful information in a prickly form.
5. **No forced bravery**: the ending proves the child can choose with confidence, not that every child must go down the slide.

## Remaining Open Questions

1. Exact page 10 invitation line: "Let's look together," "I can show you," or another safer/warmer version.
2. Exact page 17 unsafe dream image: broken bridge, too-wide jump, slippery ledge, or another not-right-for-now visual.
3. Whether page 27 should definitively show the child going down the slide or leave the chosen action slightly more open.
4. Whether page 29 should be parent-facing, child-facing, or a blend.
